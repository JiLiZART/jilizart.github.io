BBob doesn't trend. There are no breathless tweets about it, no conference talks, no weekly
newsletter mentions. It picks up about three GitHub stars a quarter, and the issue tracker is
quiet for weeks at a time.

It's also been in production at musescore.com and ultimate-guitar.com for years, parsing
millions of comments a day without complaint.

## What boring open source looks like

The release cadence is glacial — sometimes a year between minor versions. The API has barely
changed since 2018. The README is short. The test suite covers the parts that matter and skips
the rest.

There's no roadmap document. There's no Discord server. There's a small list of issues, mostly
edge cases I haven't gotten to, that I'll probably get to eventually.

## Why I keep maintaining it

Because someone, somewhere, depends on it. Because the alternative is forcing them to migrate.
Because the cost of keeping it alive is low — a few hours a quarter to triage issues and ship a
patch.

Most open source maintainers burn out chasing growth. The best move I ever made was to stop
chasing it.
