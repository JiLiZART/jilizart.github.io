I've shipped four design systems. Three of them died, slowly, by addition. Every quarter someone
needed *just one more* token, *just one more* component variant, *just one more* breakpoint.
A year in, the system was unrecognizable. Two years in, nobody used it.

The fourth one is still alive. The difference isn't better tokens. It's that I learned to say no.

## The shape of the no

It's never *no, that's a bad idea.* It's *yes, and here's the existing primitive that does it.*
Or *yes, but let's wait until two more teams need it.*

The trick is the no must come with a path forward. Otherwise you're just the design system
gatekeeper, and gatekeepers get routed around.

## Three nos I keep in my back pocket

1. **No, we already have it.** Most "new" requests are renames of existing primitives.
2. **No, but we'll revisit.** Bookmark it. If three teams ask, it's real. If one team asks, it's a one-off.
3. **No, that lives in your app.** Not everything belongs in the system. Specialty UI is fine — in your app.

Saying no is not the rude part of the job. Saying yes to everything is.
